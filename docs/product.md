# Product

Atrium addresses a practical coordination problem: hotel staff need to report what needs attention, route it to the right team and leave enough context for the next person to continue.

## A representative workflow

1. A member of staff notices a flickering corridor light.
2. They report the issue with a location and context, manually or through a voice-assisted draft.
3. The responsible department sees the work and coordinates ownership.
4. Progress and resolution become visible to colleagues and managers.
5. Managers review recorded activity alongside recurring operational tasks.

This is an illustrative scenario, not a description of a customer deployment.

## Product decisions

**A report needs usable context.** Location, department and progress are more useful to the next shift than an isolated message.

**Shared work needs explicit responsibility.** An incident can involve departmental coordination and managerial review. The public demo reduces that complexity to a single department label and a simple status cycle.

**Routine work and exceptions belong together.** Scheduled tasks complement incident reporting; they are separate concepts with different lifecycles.

**AI should reduce input effort.** The production voice flow prepares editable drafts and offers manual reporting. The demo shows a fixed example and makes no AI requests.

## Demo boundaries

The demo offers three views: overview, incident coordination and routines. All data was written specifically for this showcase. There are no real hotels, people, customer records, accounts, uploads or saved changes. Its status transitions and routine controls are illustrative interactions, not published production policies.
